Token Auction Lab · research note

What can a pair of bids buy?

Two advertisers share one generated answer. At every step a token auction mixes their preferred next steps in proportion to their bids. For one random seed, we can now list every answer any pair of bids can produce, exactly, with what each would cost.

One comparison per step

At each step the two advertisers' next-step distributions mostly agree. That overlap is sampled for free and ignores the bids. On the part they disagree about, each side proposes a candidate and one uniform random number u settles the contest: A's candidate wins exactly when u < λ, where λ = a/(a+b) is A's share of the bids. The winner pays the bid at which it would have lost, its critical bid.

The random numbers are drawn from the seed, step by step, before anyone bids. So for a fixed seed the bids enter the whole generation only through λ, one comparison at a time.

At most 1 + T answers

Follow every λ in (0, 1] at once. Start with one interval. Whenever a contested step's u falls strictly inside an interval, split it there and continue both halves. Every live interval reads the same u at step k, and the intervals never overlap, so at most one interval splits per step. A T-step answer therefore has at most 1 + T versions across all bids. Each one comes with exact charges, because within an interval every contest result is fixed.

The expected number of answers also has a clean form. Over random seeds, E[#answers] = 1 + ∫₀¹ E[#contested steps at λ] dλ, because the interval that splits at step k is the generation at λ = uk, which cannot see uk in advance. Over 2000 seeds of the city walk that averages 24.8 answers out of a possible 81.

Per token stable, per answer not

Raising your bid can only switch a contested step to your own candidate. That is the property the mechanism is built on. It does not carry over to whole answers: once one token changes, every later distribution changes too. The atlas measures this exactly for each seed instead of assuming it.

Three advertisers: a triangle of answers

With three bidders the overlap of all three distributions stays free, and contested steps become a race: each bidder draws an exponential clock Ei, the smallest Ei/bi wins, and the winner pays Ew·maxj≠w bj/Ej. For two bidders this is exactly the auction above. Bids are now a point in a triangle and answers are convex polygons. Here the 1 + T bound breaks: one contested step can cut several cells, and cell counts grow roughly quadratically.

Outcome buyers never settle

Per token, the auction is truthful: if each contested token you win is worth v credits to you, bidding v is optimal. Advertisers usually want an outcome instead, for example "the answer recommends my hotel". Averaging atlases over many seeds turns each outcome's probability and each expected charge into an exact step function of λ, so best responses can be computed exactly.

In the synthetic city walk they cycle. The café needs λ ≥ 0.67 and the bookshop λ ≤ 0.32, so each outbids the other, about ×4 per round, until one outcome stops paying for itself. That sponsor drops to almost nothing and the escalation restarts. Allocation depends only on the ratio of bids while each charge scales with the rival's level, so nothing anchors the bid level.

Exact answer atlases turn "what does this bid buy?" from a sampling question into a lookup, and they show where per-token guarantees stop.

Beyond text

Nothing here needs a language model, only two next-step distributions. Diffusion samplers qualify too. An Euler-ancestral step draws from a Gaussian whose spread is set by the noise schedule, not by the prompt, so two advertisers' prompts give two Gaussians of equal shape. These have an exact maximal coupling along the line between their means, with the same branch, contest and payments. The repository includes that coupling and an exact image atlas for SDXL, tested on tiny models. Real-model atlases for Qwen3-4B text and SDXL images are scripted but have not been run yet.

Nonclaims

Demos on this page run hand-written synthetic generators in your browser with a seeded PRNG; they are not language models. No truthful-bidding or equilibrium claim is made for whole answers; the bidding results are exploratory best-response dynamics on sampled seeds. All charges are simulated credits; nothing is billed or settled. Brands are fictional.
