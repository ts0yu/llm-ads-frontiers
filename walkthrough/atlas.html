What an atlas shows — and what it does not claim

One seed, every bid ratio

The random tape for every step (shared-or-contested draw, candidate draws, contest uniform u) is fixed by the seed before bids are known. Bids enter only through λ = a / (a + b), in one comparison per contested step: u < λ emits A's candidate, otherwise B's. So the whole answer is a step function of λ.

At most 1 + T answers

All live branches read the same u at step k, and their intervals partition (0, 1], so at most one branch splits per step. A T-step generation has at most 1 + T distinct answers over all bid ratios, deterministically. The boundaries are exactly the contest uniforms where a split happened.

Exact charges inside an answer

With S = a + b and λ inside a leaf, each contest won by A charges A S(1−λ)·u/(1−u); each won by B charges B S·λ·(1−u)/u. Shared steps are free. These are simulated credits.

Proven vs measured

Per-token stability is proven for the two-bidder construction: raising your bid can only switch a contest toward your candidate. Whether an answer-level outcome such as “mentions Bayview Hotel” is monotone in λ is not implied — the atlas measures it exactly, for this seed. A non-monotone band is a finding, not a bug.

Nonclaims

  • No truthful-bidding claim over complete answers or arbitrary advertiser utilities.
  • All charges are simulated credits; nothing is billed, sent, or settled.
  • One seed is one sample. Model numerics on other hardware can change a stored atlas.
  • Synthetic fixtures are toy data for UI work. CLIP scores are a noisy heuristic, not ground truth.
  • Three bidders use an exponential race; cells are convex, but the 1 + T bound is for two bidders only.
