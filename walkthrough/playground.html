Every outcome money can buy, exactly.

Two sponsors each have a preferred next-step distribution. The two-bidder token auction from auction.py picks each step. For one random seed, every bid ratio λ = a/(a+b) maps to one of at most 1 + T outcomes, and the boundaries between them are exactly the contest uniforms. No language model here: these are a city walk and a melody.

Three sponsors: every route, over the whole bid triangle

Each point in the triangle is a split of the bids between three shops. The N-bidder version of the auction gives the overlap of all three walking preferences away for free, then runs an exponential race on contested steps: the winner is argmin Ei/bi and pays Ew·maxj≠w bj/Ej. For one seed, every bid split inside a polygon buys exactly the same route. The 1 + T bound no longer holds with three bidders: one contested step can cut several cells.

When sponsors value outcomes, what do they bid?

The auction is truthful one step at a time: at a fixed prefix, a sponsor who values winning that contested step at v credits should bid v. Over a whole generation that guarantee is gone, because the bid also changes which later steps are contested. Sponsors usually value an outcome of the whole generation instead (the walk passes my storefront, the jingle sounds like my label). Averaging exact atlases over many seeds gives P(outcome) and expected payments as exact step functions of λ for those seeds. Here each sponsor best-responds to the other's bid, using those functions.

Fixing the cycle

Allocation depends only on the ratio λ, but each charge scales with the rival's bid level, so nothing anchors the level and best responses cycle. Here the same sponsors face modified mechanisms, all computed exactly for the sampled seeds: a reserve (a step can only be won with a bid ≥ r, and the winner pays at least r), a platform pseudo-bid (the platform bids r on the midpoint of the two distributions, so λ′ = (a + r/2)/(a + b + r)), and outcome-level mechanisms where sponsors report what their outcome is worth and the platform picks λ from the atlas. How this is exact, and what it does not show

At each step the auction splits probability mass into a common part (sampled for free, ignoring bids) and a contested part. In the contested part, A's candidate wins exactly when the step's contest uniform u is below λ. The random tape is drawn per step from the seed, never from the bids, so the whole outcome depends on the bids only through λ. Starting from λ ∈ (0, 1], the search follows one branch until a contested step whose u falls inside the current interval, then splits it at u. All live branches share the same u at a given step, so at most one of them splits per step: at most 1 + T outcomes.

Each step is stable for both bidders: raising your bid only ever switches that step to your candidate. Whole outcomes need not be monotone, and the "Outcome profiles" panel measures this exactly for this seed. A non-monotone badge means some bid increase makes that outcome less likely to occur for this seed.

Not shown or claimed: advertiser value, truthful bidding over whole outcomes, or real money. The two policies are small hand-written functions. The browser PRNG (mulberry32) is not NumPy's, so these tapes do not match Python runs. The Python atlas modules handle real models.
